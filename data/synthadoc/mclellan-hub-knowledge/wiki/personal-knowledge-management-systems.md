# Building an AI-Powered Second Brain

The source demonstrates a personal knowledge system built around three connected parts: a searchable wiki, a lightweight CRM, and a journal. Saved web material, meeting notes, people records, and reflections are stored in an Obsidian vault and made available to an AI assistant for retrieval and synthesis.

## Core Model

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Wiki | Stores articles, videos, transcripts, notes, and extracted concepts |
| CRM | Records people, encounters, conversations, and contact context |
| Journal | Uses relevant material from the wiki and CRM to support reflection |
| AI layer | Summarises sources, extracts entities, finds links, and retrieves context |

## Main Ideas

- A knowledge base is useful only if information is resurfaced during real work.
- Wiki, CRM, and journal records become more valuable when they share one retrieval layer.
- Automated ingestion can turn clipped material into concise, interlinked notes.
- Human-readable source records and provenance remain important even when AI performs the organisation.
- The structure should follow the owner’s actual activities rather than a universal second-brain template.

## Practical Workflow

1. Capture useful material from the web, meetings, conversations, or projects.
2. Convert it into concise notes with clear titles and source references.
3. Link related concepts without merging distinct subjects.
4. Search or converse with the resulting knowledge base.
5. Use journal or work context to resurface relevant prior material.
